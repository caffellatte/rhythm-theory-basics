# A practice course for the SP-404MKII

The aim is to hear a phrase, play it, program it, and change it on purpose. Keep the same musical idea as you move between live pads, Roland TR-REC, and Klangfeld Labs' DOOM OS. Start each new cycle with a fresh groove. Begin with the kick listening exercise. [Listening page](pilot/kick-placement.html) · [Print card](pilot/kick-placement.pdf)

## Four short sessions per cycle

DJ Kool Herc recalled watching dancers wait for the break and using two copies of a James Brown record to play it again. Try listening for the return to beat one each time your own loop comes around. [Kool Herc's SPIN interview, archived by the Rennie Harris American Street Dance Archive](https://rennieharrisamericanstreetdancearchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/the-spin-interview-dj-kool-herc-john-leland-1.pdf)

| Session | Work at the instrument | What to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Hear it and play it live | Tap the pulse and speak the count. Sing a pitched part or voice the drum sound. Record a live pass at a comfortable tempo before correcting it. Use QTZ Off when studying your own timing. | One take and the beat or syllable where you heard a difference. |
| 2. Rebuild it in TR-REC | Start from an empty pattern. Enter the intended attacks from memory, then compare with the live pass. Change one event and predict how it will sound before pressing play. | A reference pattern, one variation, and a count you can say for both. |
| 3. Inspect it in DOOM OS | Play the pattern and predict the position of the event you heard. Inspect it in the grid or Zoom view. Select a single note to try length, nudge, or velocity as appropriate. Audition the result against the bass. | The DOOM build, parameter you changed, and what you actually heard. |
| 4. Transfer it | On a later day, close the notes. Apply the idea to a different sound, tempo, or two-bar phrase. Record a short performance moving from the first version to the second. | A fresh take made from memory and the first action for next time. |

The live pass preserves your timing, TR-REC gives you an exact reference, and DOOM's Zoom displays sequenced note position and length. A drum tail can outlast that displayed note. Listen on both sides of the bass entrance to tell. [Roland real-time recording](https://static.roland.com/manuals/sp-404mk2_reference_v550/en-US/7920321178487947.html) · [Roland TR-REC](https://static.roland.com/manuals/sp-404mk2_reference_v550/en-US/7921805978489483.html) · [Klangfeld Labs manual](https://klangfeldlabs.com/doom-os/manual/)

## What to learn over successive cycles

Robert Hood described “Museum,” the first track he worked on for *Minimal Nation*, as a way to bring Chicago house and Detroit techno together. When you reach the genre studies, keep the kit and listen for connections between the parts you write. [Hood on “Museum”](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2019/05/robert-hood-minimal-nation-track-by-track/)

| Cycle | Musical problem | A test you can do with the book closed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pulse and subdivision | Keep a foot on four beats while speaking eighths, then sixteenths. Leave a beat silent. | Return on the next downbeat while the click is muted for a bar. |
| Placement and duration | Hear a kick start, follow its tail, and notice when the bass enters. | Identify a moved attack and a longer tail in two separate comparisons. Produce both effects with another drum. |
| Rests, ties, and bass | Sing *do* on a bass attack and sustain it over a subdivision. Tap the drum part against it. Write one short phrase back by ear. | Rebuild where the bass starts and ends, then make space for a melodic answer. |
| Accent and feel | Keep the notes fixed while changing one accent. In a separate pass compare straight and swung subdivisions. Count a triplet aloud as a reference. | Hear which change affected loudness and which affected spacing. Recreate one by hand. |
| Four genre studies | Make a hip-hop backbeat with two hat timings, a house groove with offbeat hats, a minimal techno loop that evolves by one percussion hit, and a trance phrase with offbeat bass beneath a repeating melody. | Pick two studies and use the same kit for both. Describe a rhythmic decision in each and perform a variation. |
| Arrangement | Extend a bar into a question and answer. Add or remove a layer at a phrase boundary and return to the opening. | Record a short performance with an entrance, a change, and an ending you can find again by ear. |

Keep a single log line per session. For example, “90 BPM, live pads. I meant the kick to answer on three-and. Listening back, I landed with three. Next time I’ll say the ‘and’ before playing.” This is an example entry. Your own observations become the raw material for the book.

For an optional rematch next session, have a partner choose one of yesterday's takes, or use a playback app on shuffle with the display covered. Name the change, then check the take against your log. If it is hard to hear, reduce the groove to a pulse and two sounds. Try it with another kit once you can sing the change.

At the end of each cycle, rate **hearing it**, **playing it live**, and **programming it** separately. Mark each as “with the card,” “from memory,” or “in a new groove.” If you can program the change from memory but still need the card to play it, begin the next cycle on the live pads. Keep the earlier take so you can hear what improved.
