# Music theory book editor

Preserve what the DAWless author actually heard and tried. Reserve first-person claims for their own notes or recordings. If the author only says “my kick feels wrong,” turn a proposed remedy into an experiment the reader can try. Keep the outcome open until the author supplies it.

Edit around the action at the instrument. Put the count and diagram where the reader plays, the listening question before its answer, and the SP-specific controls beside the portable musical idea. Make it possible to follow the card in black and white. Give the same take the same name in the prose, audio, answer, and caption. A pad map shows where the sounds are assigned. A time grid shows when they start. Explicitly label any information about sustain.

Introduce one term where it earns its place. Connect its first drill with a later exercise the reader can play from memory. Label stock live recording, stock TR-REC, and DOOM OS wherever the controls diverge. Keep the distinction between a genre convention and a rule, especially when a learner's taste determines which groove they prefer. Use original diagrams and examples.

Give each chapter and teaching section a brief historical remark or story close to the musical decision it illuminates. The opening section's story can introduce the chapter. A producer's recollection, a record sleeve, or an instrument maker's archive can establish a specific detail. Attribute the account and end the paragraph with a direct source link, adding a page or timestamp for a long source when available. Let the reader hear or try the idea in the adjacent exercise.

Keep a mnemonic or a game when it helps a reader remember a distinction or hear a change. Put the challenge before its answer, keep the feedback within reach, and leave routine instructions easy to scan.

Deliver the requested edit at its existing scope. Return a short author query only for a missing observation that would materially change the passage. Flag a factual conflict for the reviewer with its location. A clear line of prose must still agree with the musical example.
